## Session log - 2026-10-28 - design note on budgets and variable-time algorithms; open items I1-I8 filed

Maintainer discussion after acceptance: the two Collatz problems (R-tier per-input trace, unbounded; Q-tier one
circuit of the longest trace), trace-vs-circuit vocabulary, what the construction guarantees at the wall, the
wrapper pattern (K is semantic, B is operational). Read Jeffery / Mamindlapally / Nguetsa Tankeu, "Loop
Composition in Quantum Algorithms", arXiv:2605.07518: the lifted oracle is their straight-line case (eq. 1,
max over the domain); the step machine is a variable-time algorithm in their sense except that idle steps write
and clear `frame_t.old_pc` instead of being the identity; the fault register under a ladder of budget tokens is
the primitive Ambainis-style l2 variable-time search needs, and it is expressible against the ISA now. Full note:
`docs/notes/2026-10-28-budgets-loops-variable-time.md`. Open items below are the repo's issue list until `bd init`.

## Open items (pre-bd issue list)

- **I1 - Pebbled / checkpointed oracle realisations.** The section 5.2 construction keeps B frames. Bennett-1989
  pebbling (and the Knill-1995 strategies in Bennett.jl `src/pebble/`) trade frames for O(log B) checkpoints at
  polylog time. T6 arguably admits it as a section 5.7 transformation, but section 11 has no schedule kind to bind or
  cost a checkpoint and T2 is stated only for the full-history realisation. Spec change: name checkpointed
  realisations in section 5.7, add a schedule binding and a section 17 default (full history). Core, S1 with default.
- **I2 - Structure recovery of bounded data-dependent loops.** `qloop n while cond` needs `cond` computed before the
  loop from wires the body does not modify, so a Collatz-style exit cannot use it; the dispatch-free form is
  `qloop B_iter` with the body predicated on a "still running" flag. Give that idiom (or a `qloop n until flag`
  form) in section 4.6 / 5.7 so the specialiser does not pay per-step dispatch over the whole program. Core, S1.
- **I3 - Where B comes from.** Section 5.4 makes B a launch value. Document in the dialect spec: compiler-inferred
  bounds (affine loops, budget tables, north star section 9 item 4), host-supplied B, the wrapper pattern (K in the
  program, B >= longest wrapper trace), and the fault outcome as the documented behaviour above B. Contract.
- **I4 - Section 7.4 wording.** "One frame per executed instruction" is the reference model; an executor may realise
  the history by checkpoint + replay (BennettVM's production design) provided T4 holds. Say so, so bennett-vm does
  not read 7.4 as a mandate to store every frame. Core wording, S1.
- **I5 - Done-invariance of idle steps (variable-time readiness).** State as a property (candidate T7): on a branch
  that reached HALT at step t, every later forward step is the identity on (ins, tgt, fr, result_val, locations,
  cells) and leaves its frame at |0>. Make the step variant that controls the successor XOR and both commit
  exchanges on `not at_HALT` the reference (section 3.6 steps 3-4), add a suite-B test that an idle step is the
  identity, and add the `pc = HALT` predicate as the "done" projector in the formal model (section 14). Core.
- **I6 - Step-granular oracle interface (future).** Automatic loop composition in the sense of arXiv:2605.07518
  section 5 would need "advance oracle o by one step under control w" as an instruction; today a region call is
  atomic. Not for 0.x; record the need. Also write Ambainis-style variable-time search as an action over a ladder
  of budget tokens as a suite C/H example and compare the section 8.2 charge with the l2 bound.
- **I7 - Council round-8 S1 suggestions** (16: codex 5, muse 5, opus 6) from
  `../bennett/docs/council/isa-2026-10-28/round-8/out-*.json`, field `suggestions`. Triage into issues at `bd init`.
- **I8 - Section 3.6 "blocks" wording.** The index space text says "blocks, each contiguous" while every rule
  (k+1 successors, HALT = n, 2^P > n) indexes rows; the walkthrough and both examples use per-row indices. Fix the
  sentence. Core wording, S1.
